# 🎯 Agile Manifesto & Principles

## The Agile Manifesto (2001)

The Agile Manifesto was created by 17 software development pioneers who recognized the need for a better approach to software development. It outlines 4 core values and 12 principles.

---

## 4 Core Values of Agile

### 1. **Individuals and Interactions** > Processes and Tools
- Focus on people, not tools
- Communication is key
- Collaboration over documentation
- Example: Daily standups vs. lengthy status reports

### 2. **Working Software** > Comprehensive Documentation
- Deliver working product frequently
- Documentation supports development, doesn't replace it
- Measure progress by working software
- Example: Demo after each sprint

### 3. **Customer Collaboration** > Contract Negotiation
- Involve customers throughout development
- Respond to customer feedback
- Continuous engagement
- Example: Sprint reviews with stakeholders

### 4. **Responding to Change** > Following a Plan
- Embrace changing requirements
- Adapt quickly to market changes
- Flexibility over rigid planning
- Example: Adjusting backlog based on feedback

---

## 12 Agile Principles

### Principle 1: Customer Satisfaction
"Our highest priority is to satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software."
- Deliver early and often
- Focus on customer value
- Get feedback quickly

### Principle 2: Welcome Change
"Welcome changing requirements, even late in development. Agile processes harness change for the customer's competitive advantage."
- Change is normal
- Adapt to new information
- Use change as a competitive advantage

### Principle 3: Frequent Delivery
"Deliver working software frequently, from a couple of weeks to a couple of months, with a preference to the shorter timescale."
- Short release cycles
- Regular demos
- Continuous feedback

### Principle 4: Business & Developers Collaborate
"Business people and developers must work together daily throughout the project."
- Regular communication
- Shared goals
- Break down silos

### Principle 5: Motivated Teams
"Build projects around motivated individuals. Give them the environment and support they need, and trust them to get the job done."
- Trust team members
- Provide resources
- Remove obstacles
- Self-organizing teams

### Principle 6: Face-to-Face Communication
"The most efficient and effective method of conveying information to and within a development team is face-to-face conversation."
- Prefer direct conversation
- Use synchronous communication
- Minimize miscommunication

### Principle 7: Working Software
"Working software is the primary measure of progress."
- Focus on completed work
- Avoid vanity metrics
- Measure by what customers can use

### Principle 8: Sustainable Pace
"Agile processes promote sustainable development. The sponsors, developers, and users should be able to maintain a constant pace indefinitely."
- Avoid burnout
- Realistic planning
- Long-term productivity

### Principle 9: Technical Excellence
"Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design enhances agility."
- Code quality matters
- Refactoring
- Technical debt awareness

### Principle 10: Simplicity
"Simplicity—the art of maximizing the amount of work not done—is essential."
- Keep it simple
- Avoid over-engineering
- Minimal viable product (MVP)

### Principle 11: Self-Organizing Teams
"The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organizing teams."
- Team decides how to work
- Empowerment
- Autonomy

### Principle 12: Continuous Improvement
"At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly."
- Retrospectives
- Process improvement
- Experimentation

---

## Key Takeaways

✅ **People over Process**
✅ **Working Software over Documentation**
✅ **Customer Collaboration over Contracts**
✅ **Responding to Change over Plans**
