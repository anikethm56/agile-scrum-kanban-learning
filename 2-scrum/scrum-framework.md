# 🔄 Scrum Framework

## What is Scrum?

Scrum is a framework for managing product development using iterative and incremental practices. It's based on Agile principles and provides a structure for teams to deliver products in short cycles called "sprints."

---

## 3 Pillars of Scrum

### 1. **Transparency**
- Everyone understands the work
- Clear goals and status
- Visible progress
- Open communication

### 2. **Inspection**
- Regular reviews of work
- Sprint reviews
- Sprint retrospectives
- Inspect progress frequently

### 3. **Adaptation**
- Adjust based on inspection
- Improve processes
- Respond to feedback
- Continuous improvement

---

## Scrum Roles

### Product Owner (PO)
**Responsible for:**
- Defining product vision
- Managing product backlog
- Prioritizing features
- Accepting completed work
- Representing stakeholders

**Key Skill:** Understanding customer needs

### Scrum Master (SM)
**Responsible for:**
- Facilitating ceremonies
- Removing blockers
- Coaching team on Scrum
- Protecting team from interruptions
- Improving process

**Key Skill:** Servant leadership

### Development Team
**Responsible for:**
- Estimating work
- Committing to sprint goal
- Delivering working software
- Quality and testing
- Self-organization

**Key Characteristics:**
- 3-9 members (ideal: 5-7)
- Cross-functional
- Self-organizing
- Accountable for results

---

## Scrum Artifacts

### 1. Product Backlog
- Ordered list of features/requirements
- Maintained by Product Owner
- Prioritized by value
- Living document (changes)
- Example items:
  - User stories
  - Bug fixes
  - Technical improvements

### 2. Sprint Backlog
- Items selected for current sprint
- Tasks to complete sprint goal
- Team ownership
- Updated daily
- Sprint duration: 1-4 weeks (usually 2)

### 3. Product Increment
- Working software produced each sprint
- "Done" and ready for release
- Potentially shippable
- Sum of all completed items

## Scrum Ceremonies (Events)

### 1. Sprint Planning
**Duration:** 2 hours per week of sprint (4 hours for 2-week sprint)

**What:** Team selects backlog items for the sprint

**Participants:**
- Product Owner (explains what's needed)
- Scrum Master (facilitates)
- Development Team (estimates, commits)

**Output:** Sprint backlog + Sprint goal

**Key Questions:**
- What can we commit to this sprint?
- Why are we building this?
- How will we build it?

### 2. Daily Standup
**Duration:** 15 minutes

**When:** Same time each day

**What:** Team synchronizes daily

**Each person answers:**
1. What did I complete yesterday?
2. What will I complete today?
3. What blockers do I have?

**Purpose:** Synchronization, not status reporting

### 3. Sprint Review
**Duration:** 1 hour per week of sprint (2 hours for 2-week sprint)

**When:** End of sprint

**What:** Demo completed work to stakeholders

**Participants:**
- Entire team
- Stakeholders
- Customers

**Output:** Feedback for backlog refinement

### 4. Sprint Retrospective
**Duration:** 1 hour per week of sprint (1.5 hours for 2-week sprint)

**When:** After sprint review

**What:** Team reflects on process improvement

**Questions:**
- What went well?
- What went wrong?
- What can we improve?

**Output:** Action items for next sprint

### 5. Product Backlog Refinement
**Duration:** 2-4 hours per sprint (ongoing)

**What:** Prepare backlog items for future sprints

**Activities:**
- Break down large items
- Add details and acceptance criteria
- Re-prioritize
- Estimate

## Sprint Cycle (2-Week Example)

```
Week 1:
Monday:   Sprint Planning (4 hours)
Tuesday:  Daily standup, development
Wednesday: Daily standup, development
Thursday: Daily standup, development, refinement
Friday:   Daily standup, development

Week 2:
Monday:   Daily standup, development
Tuesday:  Daily standup, development
Wednesday: Daily standup, development
Thursday: Daily standup, development
Friday:   Daily standup, Sprint Review (2 hours), Retrospective (1.5 hours)

Next Sprint Starts Monday
```

## Key Metrics

### Velocity
- Story points completed per sprint
- Used for capacity planning
- Indicator of sustainable pace
- Example: Team completes 25 story points per sprint

### Burndown
- Remaining work each day
- Shows sprint progress
- Should trend downward

### Burnup
- Completed work each day
- Shows what's done
- Should trend upward

## Scrum Values

1. **Commitment** — Team commits to goals
2. **Focus** — One sprint at a time
3. **Openness** — Transparent communication
4. **Respect** — Value each team member
5. **Courage** — Speak truth, take risks

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **Long sprints** — Stick to 1-4 weeks, preferably 2
❌ **Overcommitting** — Be realistic with estimates
❌ **Canceling sprints** — Disrupts rhythm
❌ **Missing ceremonies** — They're essential
❌ **Scope creep** — Protect sprint from changes
❌ **Blaming** — Retrospectives are for improvement, not blame
❌ **Multiple projects** — Team focuses on one

---

## Summary

**Scrum = Structure + Discipline + Continuous Improvement**

- **3 Roles:** Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team
- **3 Artifacts:** Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Product Increment
- **5 Ceremonies:** Planning, Standup, Review, Retrospective, Refinement
- **Time-boxed sprints:** 1-4 weeks
- **Measure by:** Working software, velocity, team health
